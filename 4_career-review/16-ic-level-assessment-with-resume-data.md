## Honest caveats (so you can pre-empt them in interviews)

1. **Volunteer discount.** Some hiring managers reflexively weight unpaid work lower. Counter with the *scale numbers* (45k attendees, 25 people, multi-year budget) and *outcomes* (zero critical failures × 5) — those read identically to paid work. The CM retreat talk is proof a real employer already took it seriously.
2. **Mostly IT/ops-flavoured, with a product-software origin.** The bulk of SMASH (the leadership years) augments *leadership, breadth, SRE/infra, incident command* — not recent product-code depth. The exception is the founding **Software Developer** stint (2018–20), where you designed and built the ticketing + mobile-scanning system — that *is* product software and is worth naming, though it's now dated. Net: pitch SMASH as "engineering leadership + reliability at scale, built on a hands-on engineering foundation"; keep your *current* product-software seniority story anchored in CM/WiseTech.
3. **The bandwidth question.** A 40-hr/week side commitment invites "will you have capacity / is this sustainable / could it conflict?" Have a clean answer (SMASH is seasonal-peak with a year-round cadence you've run for 6 years; it's evidence of capacity, not a competing claim on it).
4. **Title inflation risk.** "Director of IT" for a volunteer convention vs. "Director" at a 500-person SaaS aren't the same band. Lead with scope and outcomes, let the title sit alongside — don't lead with the title into a software-IC interview.
