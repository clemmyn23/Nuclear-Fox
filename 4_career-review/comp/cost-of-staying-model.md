# The Cost of Staying — a back-of-envelope model

You're at **$121k base**. The comp brief puts a market reset for your level at roughly **$160–175k**. Here's what the gap costs you if you stay vs. move — so the decision is a number, not a vibe.

## Assumptions (change these to taste)
- **Stay scenario:** $121k, growing **3%/yr** (typical CM-style annual raise; you've said promos are frozen).
- **Move scenario:** reset to **$160k** now, then **5%/yr** (mix of raises + the occasional job change that keeps you near market).
- Base salary only — ignores equity/bonus, which usually *widens* the gap further at product/tech companies.
- Illustrative, pre-tax, AUD.

## Year-by-year (base salary)

| Year | Stay (3%/yr) | Move (reset $160k, 5%/yr) | Gap that year | Cumulative gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Now | $121,000 | $160,000 | $39,000 | $39,000 |
| +1 | $124,630 | $168,000 | $43,370 | $82,370 |
| +2 | $128,369 | $176,400 | $48,031 | $130,401 |
| +3 | $132,220 | $185,220 | $52,999 | $183,400 |
| +4 | $136,187 | $194,481 | $58,294 | $241,694 |
| +5 | $140,272 | $204,205 | $63,932 | $305,627 |

**Headline:** staying costs you roughly **$39k in year one alone**, and about **$300k of cumulative base salary over five years** — before equity. The gap *grows* every year, because percentage raises on a low base never catch a market reset (3% of $121k < 5% of $160k, and the spread compounds).

## The compounding trap, stated plainly
- A raise is a *percentage of your current salary*. Staying low means even "good" raises are small in dollars.
- Superannuation is a % of salary too — the gap quietly extends into your retirement contributions.
- Every year under market also lowers your *next* anchor if you ever do disclose — though the comp brief tells you not to.

## What it would take to justify staying
Staying is only rational if CM offers something the $300k buys elsewhere:
- A **concrete, written, near-term** promotion + market adjustment (not "room to grow"). — *Per your account, frozen. Unlikely.*
- Equity/bonus that closes the gap. — *Check, but unlikely at the carve-up stage.*
- Non-comp value you genuinely can't get elsewhere (rare team, unique learning, lifestyle fit). — *Only you can price this.*

If none of those are real and imminent, the model says what the prior sessions said: **the gap is large, compounding, and only a move closes it.**

## A gentler framing (because money isn't everything)
This isn't "quit tomorrow." It's: the cost of *staying without a plan* is ~$39k/year and rising. Even spending 3–6 months interviewing while employed — using the kit in this folder — costs you nothing and is expected to return tens of thousands per year for years. The expected value of *just running the search* is enormous relative to its effort.

## Caveats (read these)
- These are **scenarios on estimates**, not predictions. Your real reset, raises, and offers will differ.
- Job-hopping has costs too (ramp-up, risk, vesting cliffs, culture lottery — which is why `interview/reverse-interview-questions.md` exists).
- I'm not a financial adviser; this is a thinking tool, not advice. Plug in your own numbers.
