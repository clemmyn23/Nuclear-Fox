# The Negotiation: A Script in Five Beats
### *or, How the Senior Engineer Reclaimed His Market Rate*

> *Dramatis personae:*
> **YOU** — a senior engineer, 8 years deep, underpaid at $121k, finally interviewing.
> **THE RECRUITER** — friendly, well-meaning, armed with a budget and instructions to spend as little of it as possible.
> **THE HIRING MANAGER** — appears in Beat IV; wants you, has more power than the recruiter admits.
> *Setting: a video call. Somewhere off-screen, your current employer's C-suite is being replaced for the fifth time.*

---

## BEAT I — The Anchor Trap
*Enter THE RECRUITER, bearing a lowball wrapped in enthusiasm.*

**RECRUITER:** "We're really excited! Before we go further — just so we're aligned — what are you currently earning?"

**YOU:** *(pleasant, unbothered)* "I'd rather not anchor on my current number — honestly it's below market, and correcting that is part of why I'm looking. Based on my research, senior engineers with my experience in Sydney are in the **$160–185k base** range, and that's where I'm focused. What's the band for this role?"

> 🗡️ **The move:** You refused to hand over the weapon that would be used against you. In Australia you're not obliged to disclose current pay. You *re-anchored* on the market, not your history, and bounced the question back so **they** reveal the band first. Never let your old salary become the ceiling.

---

## BEAT II — The Range Reveal
**RECRUITER:** "Got it. The band for this level is **$140k to $165k** base."

**YOU:** "That's a workable range. Given I'm operating at senior scope already — I led a customer-data feature end-to-end through architecture review — and I separately run a 25-person engineering org, I'd be expecting the **upper half**. I'm targeting **$175k**; where we land depends on the total package."

> 🗡️ **The move:** You accepted the band without flinching, then *anchored at the top of it (slightly above)* and justified it with two concrete differentiators. $175 against a $165 ceiling isn't greedy — it gives them room to "win" by settling at $165 while you still clear your real floor.

---

## BEAT III — The Lowball Gambit
*THE RECRUITER produces a number, gift-wrapped.*

**RECRUITER:** "We'd love to bring you in at **$148k**. It's a great number for the level, and there's room to grow."

**YOU:** *(warm, not rattled)* "I appreciate it, and I'm genuinely keen on the team. But $148k undersells the fit — I'm bringing senior delivery *and* real leadership experience most candidates at this level don't have. Can we get to the **top of the band on base**, or close the gap with **equity or a sign-on**? I want to say yes."

> 🗡️ **The move:** Three things at once — (1) reaffirm enthusiasm so it stays collaborative, not combative; (2) reject the number without rejecting *them*; (3) open **multiple levers** (base / equity / sign-on) so a stuck base isn't a dead end. "There's room to grow" is the oldest line in the book — you've lived five years of that promise unkept, so you anchor on *now*, not "later."

---

## BEAT IV — The Hidden Lever *(competing offer — only if true)*
*Enter THE HIRING MANAGER, who actually wants you.*

**YOU:** "I'll be straight with you because I'd rather join you than them: I have another offer at **$170k base**. If you can match or beat the total package, I'll sign with you this week."

**HIRING MANAGER:** *(to recruiter, off-mic)* "...can we find another twenty?"

> 🗡️ **The move:** A *real* competing offer is the single most powerful lever in the game — it converts "budget" into "found money" with startling speed. Note the framing: *"I'd rather join you"* lowers their risk of losing you while raising the stakes. **Only deploy if true** — a bluff that gets called ends the quest. (This is why the brief tells you to interview in *batches*: so offers land together and this beat is available.)

---

## BEAT V — The Close
**RECRUITER:** "Okay — we can do **$168k base**, plus the standard equity and a $5k sign-on."

**YOU:** "That works. Send it through in writing and I'll confirm. Thank you — I'm looking forward to it." *(You do NOT resign anywhere until the signed offer is in hand.)*

> 🗡️ **The move:** You stopped negotiating once you'd cleared your target — squeezing the last dollar after a fair offer risks the relationship you're about to depend on. **Get it in writing before you tell anyone you're leaving.** Quest complete: **$121k → $168k**, a ~39% raise, in one move that five years of waiting never produced.

---

## Appendix — lines to keep in your back pocket
- **"What's the budget for this role?"** (deflect any 'what do you want' opener back to them)
- **"I'm weighing a few conversations, so I want to make sure the level and number are right up front."** (creates gentle urgency, true if you batched)
- **"Is the level negotiable, or just the comp within it?"** (sometimes the *title* flexes — Senior→Lead — which moves the whole band)
- **"Can we revisit comp at 6 months rather than 12?"** (a lever when base is genuinely capped)
- **The walk-away line (calm, final):** "I don't think the level and package reflect the role, so I'll step back — but I really enjoyed meeting the team." (Walking is a move; sometimes the number reappears.)

## The one rule
This only works if you have **options** (real interviews in flight) and the willingness to walk. The leverage isn't a clever line — it's that you genuinely don't need *this* offer. Get to that position first; the script is just how you spend it.
