## Market context (Sydney, AUD, base — ESTIMATES, validate)

| Band | Typical base range | Where it's found |
|---|---|---|
| Mid SWE (SE2) | ~$100–135k | most companies |
| **Senior SWE (SE3)** | **~$140–185k** | product cos, scale-ups, banks |

**Total comp** at top-tier (equity + bonus) pushes senior well past base alone — senior total comp in the $200–300k range exists at the Atlassian/Canva/big-tech tier. Banks (CommBank, Macquarie, Westpac, NAB) pay strong base, lighter equity. Agencies/smaller SaaS sit lower.

**Read on your number:** $121k for 8 YoE doing senior work is roughly **$25–65k below the senior base range**, before equity. The gap is real and large enough that only a job change resets it (annual raises don't close 30–50%).

## Your target number

Most senior-IC candidates can't show people leadership. You ran a 25-person org and set Board-level strategy. That makes you credible for **Lead/Staff/EM** postings, which sit a band higher. Apply to a mix — don't cap yourself at senior-IC.

> **Tenure note:** this leadership record isn't recent or shallow — it's a **~7.5-year arc (Nov 2018–present), founding engineer → Director of IT**, sustained alongside the day job. That longevity is itself a negotiation asset: it's proven, not a one-off.

## Negotiation script

### Rule 0: never give your current salary first
In Australia you're not obliged to disclose it. If asked:
> "I'd rather not anchor on my current number — it's below market and I'm specifically correcting that with this move. Based on my research, senior engineers with my experience in Sydney are in the **$160–185k base** range, and that's where I'm focused. What's the band for this role?"

### When they ask "what are your expectations?" (anchor)
> "For a senior role with my 8 years and full-stack + leadership background, I'm targeting **$175k base** plus equity. I'm flexible on the mix depending on the total package."

*(Anchor high-but-reasonable; let them counter. $175 anchor → likely settles $160–170.)*

### When they lowball (e.g. "$140k is our max for the level")
> "I appreciate that. Given I'm already doing senior-scoped work — leading a feature end-to-end through architecture review, and separately running a 25-person engineering org — I think the level and number undersell the fit. Can we look at either the senior+ band, or close the base gap with equity/sign-on?"

Levers to trade if base is stuck: **sign-on bonus, equity, title (Senior→Lead), review-cycle timing (6-month not 12), WFH/leave.**

### When you have competing offers (best leverage)
> "I have another offer at **$X base**. I'd prefer to join you — can you match or improve on the total package?"
*(Only say this if true. A real competing offer is the single strongest lever; aim to interview in batches so offers land together.)*

### Closing / when to walk
- Get it **in writing** before resigning anywhere.
- Walk if: base is below ~$145k for a senior title with no equity to compensate, OR they won't put the title in writing, OR the process felt like the same dysfunction you're leaving.

## Tactics that actually move the number
1. **Interview in batches** so 2–3 offers arrive within ~2 weeks → real leverage.
2. **Apply one band up** (Lead/Staff/EM) where the posting allows — your SMASH record qualifies you and the bands pay more.
3. **Let recruiters name a number first**; if pushed, anchor at $175k.
4. **Separate "level" from "money"** — sometimes the title is fixed but base/equity/sign-on flex. Push every lever, not just base.
5. **Validate the ranges** above with levels.fyi (limited AU), Glassdoor, Blind, and 2–3 recruiters before you anchor — treat my numbers as a starting map, not gospel.

## Honest caveats
- I'm not a recruiter and these are estimates, not a guaranteed market. Sydney comp data is thinner than US data.
- Volunteer leadership (SMASH) gets variable credit — lead with its *scope/outcomes*, not the title, into IC interviews.
- Your stack (.NET/C# + TS/React) is in steady demand in Sydney (banks, logistics, SaaS); it's a strong, employable combination.
