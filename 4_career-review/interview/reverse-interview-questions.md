# Reverse-Interview Questions — screen the employer back

You've been burned by leadership churn, a promo/comp freeze, and being under-levelled for years. Don't walk into the same thing. These questions let you diagnose it *before* you accept. Ask different ones to different interviewers (peer, hiring manager, skip/director).

## Tier 1 — the ones that matter most for your situation

### On leadership stability (your #1 burn — 5 years of C-suite churn)
- **"How stable has the senior leadership / exec team been over the last 2–3 years? Any recent reorgs or changes in direction?"**
  - 🟢 specific, calm answer with continuity. 🔴 vague, nervous laughter, "lots of change but it's exciting" → same chaos.
- **"Has the company been through an acquisition or ownership change recently? How did that affect engineering?"**
  - You lived the Marigold/Zeta carve-up; you know the symptoms. Listen for whether eng was shielded or whipsawed.

### On promotion & levelling rigour (your #2 burn — frozen, under-levelled)
- **"How does promotion work here — is there a defined ladder, a calibration process, and a regular cycle? When did someone on this team last get promoted?"**
  - 🟢 named ladder, fixed cadence, a recent concrete example. 🔴 "it's case-by-case / when budget allows / we don't really have levels" → you'll freeze again.
- **"If I join as a [Senior], what does the path to [Staff/Lead] look like, and roughly how long does it take in practice?"**
  - 🟢 they can describe it. 🔴 hand-waving → no real track.
- **"How are levels decided at offer stage — is there a rubric I can see?"**
  - Tests whether levelling is principled or political (you've been on the wrong end of political).

### On comp transparency
- **"Are there defined salary bands per level? Do you do regular market adjustments, or is comp mostly set at hire?"**
  - 🟢 bands + periodic review. 🔴 "set at hire, raises are small/rare" → exactly your current trap; you'd need to job-hop again to fix comp.

## Tier 2 — engineering culture & whether your strengths are valued

- **"How are technical decisions made — is there an architecture/design-review process, and who owns it?"** (You author LDRs; check if there's a real venue to *own* architecture here — your Staff gap.)
- **"How does code review work, and how much does the org invest in shared tooling / platform / standards?"** (Your JS-crew strength — is it valued or invisible here?)
- **"What's the IC vs management split? Can a senior IC keep growing without becoming a manager?"** (Protects your two-door optionality.)
- **"How do you balance shipping speed against long-term maintainability?"** (You'll hear instantly whether it's a feature-factory or an eng org.)
- **"What does on-call / incident response look like, and how is reliability owned?"** (You've run zero-failure live ops; see if they're mature or chaotic.)

## Tier 3 — day-to-day & people

- **"What's the tenure like on this team — and why did the last person who left, leave?"** (Attrition reasons are very revealing.)
- **"What would you change about engineering here if you could?"** (A non-answer is a red flag; honesty is a green one.)
- **"How is impact recognised beyond shipping features — e.g. mentoring, reviews, docs, platform work?"** (Your multiplier work is currently invisible; check if it counts here.)
- **"What does success look like for this role in the first 6–12 months?"** (Clarity = a well-run org.)

## How to deploy
- Ask **Tier 1 of the hiring manager and the skip-level/director** — they own the answers and can't deflect.
- Ask **Tier 2 of your would-be peers** — peers tell the truth about culture.
- Spread them out; don't interrogate. 2–3 sharp questions per interviewer.
- **Red-flag math:** if promotion is "case-by-case," comp is "set at hire," AND leadership just churned — that's your current job with a new logo. Walk.
- Green-flag signal you're specifically hunting for: a **named ladder + a recent real promotion + market comp reviews**. That's the antidote to the last five years.

## A note for you
You're not just being evaluated — after five years of dysfunction you've *earned* the right to evaluate hard. Asking these well also signals seniority: candidates who probe org health read as people who've operated at a level where it matters.
