# "Tell me about yourself": opening scripts (2026-11)

The first question sets the frame for the whole loop. Feedback said interviewers weren't hearing **ownership**, so the opener now plants it in the first 20 seconds. Structure: **who I am → what I lead (with an outcome) → the leadership extra → why here**.

## 30-second version (IC roles): your default
> "I'm a senior full-stack engineer, eight-plus years, currently at Campaign Monitor. These days I lead customer-facing features end to end. Most recently I led the technical side of our new SMS onboarding, from discovery through architecture to real-customer testing, replacing a manual support process. Before that I owned the builder canvas and launch readiness for Drip Campaigns, which now ships on every plan. I'm hands-on across React, C#/.NET and event-driven back ends. Outside work I'm Director of IT for one of Australia's biggest conventions, leading a 25-person team. I'm looking for a senior role where I own a product area and its outcomes."

## 30-second version (Lead / EM roles)
> "I'm an engineer who leads. At Campaign Monitor I'm the person who gets handed the ambiguous, high-priority feature. This year that was SMS onboarding, where I wrote the plan, staged the rollout, proposed the team split my lead adopted, and took it to dev-complete. For seven years I've also run IT for SMASH!, one of Australia's largest conventions, from founding engineer to Director of IT: 25 people, three leads, 45,000 attendees, and five years without a critical failure. I'm looking for a role that uses both: technical leadership with real ownership of outcomes."

## 2-minute version (IC): present → proof → past → why
> **Present (lead with ownership):** "I'm a senior engineer at Campaign Monitor, the email-marketing platform. My last few months are a good example of how I work. Our team had just merged and our tech lead had left. One of Product's highest priorities was letting Sales sell SMS, but onboarding a customer was a manual process only one person knew. My manager asked me to own discovery. I wrote the technical plan the PM ran with, documented the sales-led-first rollout, and made sure we staged it, because carrier registration can't be undone. I designed the provisioning API so a retry could never buy a second phone number, and got a partner team to change their API to unblock us. We went from blank page to testing with real customers in about two months. ⟨+ adoption number when you have it⟩."
>
> **Proof it's a pattern:** "Before that I owned the frontend and the launch for Drip Campaigns, our branching email sequences. I built the canvas, defended the database change for routing those sends, and wrote the go-live runbook our outgoing tech lead named the template for others. It's on every plan now. Earlier I was feature tech lead on CRM Lite, a single-customer-view feature I took through architecture review."
>
> **Past:** "Before Campaign Monitor I was at WiseTech, where as a junior I solely owned a real-time ship-tracking system used by every CargoWise customer. Ownership running ahead of title has been the pattern. Alongside all of it I've spent seven years with SMASH!, one of Australia's biggest conventions. I started as one of five founding engineers on the ticketing system, and now I'm Director of IT leading a 25-person team."
>
> **Why here:** "I'm looking for a senior role where I own a product area end to end, from the roadmap conversation through to the numbers after launch. ⟨One specific, genuine reason this company/role fits: product, tech, mission or team⟩."

## What NOT to open with any more
- ❌ "I'm the de-facto reviewer / I'm in the JavaScript Crew / I upgraded webpack, TypeScript and React." True, but it reads as **"tooling person"**, which is the "pet projects" note from the feedback. Save it for "how do you raise the bar for the team?"
- ❌ Breadth lists ("six product domains, 43 repos"). Breadth is a mid-level signal. **Depth of ownership** is the senior signal.
- ❌ "We built…" for the whole answer. Use "I" for what you decided and "we" for what the team built.

## Delivery notes
- Name **one decision** in the opener (sales-led rollout or idempotent API). It invites the follow-up you *want*.
- Have the SMASH line ready to expand or drop. Interviewers usually lean in, but in an IC loop keep it to one sentence until they ask.
- Don't badmouth Campaign Monitor. If asked why you're leaving: "I've grown into leading features and I want a role where that's the job, with the scope and title to match."
- Time yourself: the 30s versions should be **25–35 seconds**, the 2-min version **under 2:15**.
